Acupuncture

Tradition, practice and research

Acupuncture combines a long therapeutic tradition with modern clinical research. This page presents its history, the setting of a session, the use of needles, and what scientific institutions describe about studied benefits and safety.

Origins and development

A tradition without a single inventor

Acupuncture has no single recognized inventor. Its roots are in traditional Chinese medicine, and its development took place over many centuries. NCCIH notes that some form of acupuncture has been used for at least 2,500 years.

The Huangdi Neijing , or Inner Classic of the Yellow Emperor, is an important historical work within Chinese medical tradition. Huangdi is therefore presented as a cultural and historical reference, not as a proven individual inventor of acupuncture.

01 · Acupuncture

History and cultural context

Acupuncture is part of traditional Chinese medicine and developed over centuries. The practice uses thin needles inserted at specific body points within different schools and systems of interpretation. In contemporary healthcare, it has also been investigated through clinical methods, creating a bridge between tradition and experimental research.

02 · Acupuncture

How a session may take place

A session may begin with a discussion of the person’s concerns, history and goals. The practitioner then selects points and inserts the needles, which remain in place for a defined period. The number of points and techniques vary. The setting should support hygiene, privacy and clear communication, and the materials used should be appropriate for the procedure.

03 · Acupuncture

What research investigates

NCCIH reports that studies investigate acupuncture’s effects on the nervous system, tissues near insertion points and nonspecific factors such as expectations and the practitioner-patient relationship. Clinical research also examines different conditions, especially pain. Results depend on the condition studied and on the design of each study.

04 · Acupuncture

Evidence in context

For pain, systematic reviews and meta-analyses have found favorable effects in some contexts, but the magnitude of benefit and comparisons with different controls vary across studies. NCCIH also notes that how acupuncture works is not fully understood. A responsible presentation therefore avoids universal promises and explains that evidence is specific to each investigated use.

05 · Acupuncture

Safety and quality of the practice

WHO has published benchmarks for acupuncture practice and training, including procedures, facilities and safety elements. Because the technique breaks the skin, hygiene, appropriate materials and training are central. People taking medicines that affect clotting, with specific conditions or who are pregnant should inform the practitioner and follow individualized guidance.

Care and limits · Acupuncture

Restrictions and care during a session

Session quality depends on hygiene, appropriate materials, and clear communication. Needles should be sterile and disposable when required for the procedure. It is important to tell the practitioner about medicines, bleeding risks, pregnancy, and other relevant conditions so the practice can be adapted safely.

  • Check hand, room, and material hygiene.
  • Mention medicines, health conditions, and special situations before treatment.
  • Seek healthcare when symptoms or clinical needs require professional assessment.
